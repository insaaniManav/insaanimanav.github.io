Recently a few of my juniors bought the BambuLab A1, It looked expensive and felt like one of those Ultimaker printers you take out of the box and they deliver perfect prints from day 1.

Even the print quality was insane. Multi extruder setup, inbuild auto levelling. Their dynamic flow control all of it felt magical. All of that close to 30k too. So I went down the rabbit-hole if it's even worth buying an Ender3 today.

My Ender3-v2 turned 5 this year,  I got this wayy back in 2021 for 18k when 3d printers were just becoming affordable. A bit out of curiousity but mostly out of FOMO and some peer pressure.

All my friends seem to have one and I wanted to see what the fuss what about.

Now one thing I learnt very early on when I first got it was, not every Creality printer is born the same. Hell not even 2 printers packed side by side in the exact same factory will behave the same.

When you first set up your Ender 3, its young and fast and will print mostly anything you want it to.

Your first tryst with repairing it eventually happens when the stock plastic extruder snaps on you, and that's the also the time you realize that this printer you have, you're the only one who has to raise it and if you're lucky some reddit strangers online who will might be able to help you.

You go on Amazon find a nice metal extruder, Oh replacements don't really cost that much. It arrives you then spend half a day with your first printer repair ever and that's when the machine starts feeling like your own.

I myself over the course of owning this have had a few points where I just wanted to rage sell my Ender3. This close to putting it on OLX. But something in me just wanted to keep going. 

I had a whole phase where no matter what I did, the first layer just wouldn't stick for days, across multiple prints and designs. I would spend the entire day levelling the bed and not a single layer would stick. Got a CR-touch set it up, reflashed the firmware. Helped barely. Replaced the nozzle and voila . Even on the worst levelling it started to stick.

Or the time when prints would fail mid-way sometimes, Random nozzle clogs, mid print. Spent weeks debugging it. Turns out the hotend fan had gotten super weak. Got a new hotend fan. Fixed voila.

Most recently I had been struggling with strange displacement of my X-axis. The first few layers would be fine but after a while the X-Axis ever so slightly started getting displaced leading to strange artifacts on my prints that nobody on reddit could figure out. Got new timing belts for X and Y axis. Fixed

Got your point Manav, Ender 3 is a bad printer, that's why you should've gotten a BambuLab. 

I thought that too until I started digging into replacements and repairs of Bambu printers.

Stumbled into this fun little thread - https://forum.bambulab.com/t/the-right-to-repair-our-printers-is-it-just-an-illusion/201893 

A user had a fried mainboard, They ordered the replacement, opened it up, replaced the motherboard and the printer just won't work still. As it turns out support had to activate their mainboard so they can use it - https://forum.bambulab.com/t/ap-board-replacement/201826/2 ?? 

I realized while thinking this through, the same Apple vs Android argument could be made here, A BambuLab printer is just well made, it just won't need that much repair. Well it's not really a smartphone is it(I doubt this argument even works on smartphones, a post for another time though). It's a CNC machine. With the same failure modes as any FDM printer.

Same steppers, Same timing belts, Similar mainboard, similar hotend assembly. At it's core a 3d printer remains the same, It will need the same amount of upkeep and repairs as any other printer on the market and I feel after my little research Bambu intentionally makes that harder.

I realized that all the repairs I had to make weren't the cost of owning a cheap or worse printer, they're how I got to know this machine.

I know if I want to print a print-in-place part what flow rate I need.

I know what kind of first layer speed I need to get the first layer to stick on intricate patterns.

I know exactly which parts will need supports which won't.

If tomorrow, my Ender3s mainboard dies, I go online, get a new mainboard, wire it up and be printing in the next minute. I don't need to fight a battle with Creality support.

It works the other way round too btw , Creality support won't care if my printer makes a strange sound or even catches on fire.

I was never sold a printer. I was sold some extrusions, steppers and allen keys. I am the one who turned it into a printer.
