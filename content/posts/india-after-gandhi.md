Disclaimer 2: This is not a political blog. I do not claim to know what politics is or how it works. I barely know enough civics to understand various parts of our economy. My effort to read these political books is to bridge gaps in my understanding and form a solid opinion of my own instead of letting my Instagram or Twitter(Formerly X) feed do it for me

So this book found me when I was browsing around OM book store at the Unity One Elegante Mall in NSP. The cover and the title seemed fairly interesting but my biggest fear was that I would be buying a history textbook.

Asked the price at the bookstore, checked amazon and then finally did what every sane person who wants a book does. Wait until Sunday and get it pre-loved at Mahila Haat for like 1/3rd the price and a torn cover.

From the get go this book seemed fairly interesting to me. Before starting this I went down a shallowish rabbit hole of who Guha (The author) is. 

I learnt this little trick when I read This book called the Lady In The Red Sari by Javier Moro. This book was about Sonia Gandhi but the way Mr Moro puts it, it becomes more about Indira and by the end of it, I ended up actually empathizing with Indira during even her worst times and policies.

I like the initial parts about Nehru and the foundation of India, only when you read what Vallabhbhai Patel and VP Menon did to unite 500 odd pieces of land together is when you realize why he's called *Sardar(The leader)* Vallabhbhai Patel

Or when you actually look at what all happened during the Emergency you appreciate how that period was truly a dark period for our Nation. Most folks would say that it was caused because of the Supreme Court verdict on Indira Gandhi but not enough know or talk about how JP Narayan and his protests in Bihar were also key to the emergency.

After reading this I realized why my grandmother votes the way she does or why my parents vote the way they do.

He obviously glosses over a lot of (subjectively) negative things Nehru did like AFSPA and how its still going in the Northeast, Other Adivasi issues like their movements, protests were also lacking.

Overall though for someone who had spent the last 25 years in the country without knowing the lore from a good (maybe biased) source, this was a fun informative read.
