This one had been collecting dust on my shelf for a while. Had this guy's face staring right at me every time I opened my Almirah, Taunting me with all its 500 pages.
Well guess what, this was finally the year I got the attention span to read 500 page books.

Before we start it is important to know who the writer of this blog post is, just so we get my bias out of the way. 

A few months after I started college back in 2019, the world just kinda shutdown. We were all bound to our homes and while people made strange coffee or hoarded face masks, I found myself in a Local Linux Users Group.

From there somehow I started as a sysadmin At Software Freedom Law Center, Delhi and then it was a quick descent into the bowels of Indian Free Software Community joining FSCI, Even mailing with Richard Stallman and I think I was
helping in campaigning for a [Political party](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/indian-pirates-party-all-set-its-virgin-assembly-polls-kerala-41939) at some point.

So if it isn't apparent already, I grew up in college, fairly Anti-Apple. Pro Steve Wozniak. Not really Pro Steve Jobs.

---

## My take on Apple Products

The hacker in me could never get past the fact that I couldn't sideload or get a RAM upgrade on one of these things.

I even gave them a fair shot, Got an iPhone 13, a hand me down Apple Watch, AirPods Pro, company MacBook. The whole spiel.

After a few months I didn't feel suffocated because I wasn't able to sideload, I learnt to live with Youtube Ads and other things, I suffocated from the lack of options.

If I feel the AirPods' sound isn't good enough and I want to buy a pair of Sony's, well they won't work as well. Only half the features will work.
Mind you, not because Sony doesn't want them to work well with Apple products, It's because Apple doesn't want them to work well with their phones. 

If I want to get a phone with a bigger camera sensor than the ones Apple sell. Well tough luck, You can go buy an Android and just make sure your Apple watch turns to a brick.

Well eventually, sold the phone, AirPods and watch off and came back home to an S23 Ultra, Sony XM4s, Galaxy Watch 2 within 6 months.

Of all my criticism I have to give Apple one thing, their Macbooks are just wonder-laptops. The M-series chips are just absolute beasts with a capital B, the most battery efficient, fast chips I have ever used in a Laptop.

But still I would say this is true only for my work Laptop, My personal computer continues to be a Ryzen 7 PC I built with my bare hands a few years ago. It'll probably outlive any other Mac I ever get. I can just order RAM off Amazon or a new CPU and the PC will be new. For my personal systems I still prefer something I can tinker with in my free-time. For my work laptop it just has to work so it has to be a Mac I guess.

I could potentially rant about Apple for hours but this [16 year old article](https://boingboing.net/2010/04/02/why-i-wont-buy-an-ipad-and-thi.html) by Cory Doctorow has this wonderful quote I would like to add here that sums it all up pretty well

> Buying an iPad for your kids isn't a means of jump-starting the realization that the world is yours to take apart and reassemble; it's a way of telling your offspring that even changing the batteries is something you have to leave to the professionals.

Turns out the walled garden that suffocated me was this man's plan from Day 1.

## About the Book

The author himself is just a biographer, He's not Jobs' best friend or mortal enemy and his writing style reflects that for the most part. He makes no effort to make you sympathize with him.

Steve Jobs was not a good manager, neither was he a good CEO, I mean from my read of the book he was barely a decent human being.

Throughout the book he is painted as someone who was extremely passionate about what he wanted to build and hence that is where his eccentric behaviour stems from.

He used to randomly scream at people in meetings, tell them their ideas or work were shit, then come back and take credit for those ideas if he sat on them and realized they weren't shit.

He abandoned a daughter and paid the estranged mother off with a much smaller amount right before IPO.

He did also bring the world the iPod, the Macintosh, the iPhone and the iPad, Toy Story 1, 2, 3. Pixar studios.

What I do like about the book is the above facts are all represented in a linear manner, one after the other, all of them given the same importance. The goods and bads both together

--- 

## Verdict

Finally the book was a decent read, It's for Visionaries, for Dreamers,  for IIT graduated Product Managers who want to "Inspire" their Engineers to spend another night working on their "World-Changing" feature which is going to bring in infinite revenue (Sorry for the tangent(Or Am I)).

Pick it up if you want to know more about the life of this Man, whether you idolize him or despise him, you will find enough examples to support your claim either way.
