---

The author himself is just a biographer, He's not Jobs' best friend or mortal enemy and his writing style reflects that for the most part. He makes no effort to make you sympathize with him.

But it's important to know who the writer of this blog post is probably before we go ahead,
When I started college back in 2019, the world had just started understanding what COVID-19 was. We were all bound to our homes and while people made strange coffee or hoarded face masks, I found myself in a Local Linux Users Group.

From there I have no idea how but I started a job as a sysadmin At Software Freedom Law Center, Delhi and then it was a quick descent into the bowels of Indian Free Software Community joining FSCI, Even mailing with Richard Stallman and I think I was
helping in campaigning for a [Political party](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/indian-pirates-party-all-set-its-virgin-assembly-polls-kerala-41939) at some point.

So if it isn't apparent already, I grew up in college, fairly Anti-Apple. Pro Steve Wozniak. Not really Pro Steve Jobs.

--- 

Now that we have established the tone of the post, Let's dive in.

Steve Jobs was not a good manager, neither was he a good CEO, I mean from my read of the book he was barely a decent human being.

He is painted as someone who was extremely passionate about what he wanted to build and hence that is where his eccentric behaviour stems from.

He used to randomly scream at people in meetings, tell them their ideas or work were shit, then come back and take credit for those ideas if he sat on them and realized they weren't shit.

He abandoned a daughter and paid the estranged mother off with a much smaller amount right before IPO.

He did also bring the world the iPod, the Macintosh, the iPhone and the iPad, Toy Story 1, 2, 3. Pixar studios.

What I do like about the book is the above facts are all represented in a linear manner, one after the other, all of them given the same importance. The goods and bads both together

--- 

My take on apple products for the longest time was Anti. The hacker in me could never get past the fact that I couldn't sideload or get a RAM upgrade on one of these things.

I even gave them a fair shot, Got an iPhone 13, a hand me down Apple Watch, AirPods Pro, company MacBook. The whole spiel.

After a few months I didn't feel suffocated because I wasn't able to sideload, I learnt to live with Youtube Ads and other things, I suffocated from the lack of options.

If I feel the AirPods' sound isn't good enough and I want to buy a pair of Sony's, well they won't work as well. Only half the features will work.
If I want to get a phone with a bigger camera sensor and Apple doesn't sell one, I can't if I want my AirPods and watch to work with it.

Sold the phone, AirPods and watch off and came back home to an S23 Ultra, Sony XM4s, Galaxy Watch 2 within 6 months.

One thing I never got off using, and something I love using till date is their MacBooks. The M-series chips are just absolutely wonderful chips, the most battery efficient, fast chips I have ever used in a Laptop.

Another thing I like about the MacBook is their ability to just work for years on end, When buying a MacBook cost is something that I never think of. Boy math says if a 3.5 Lakh Rupee laptop lasts you 6 years that's like 50k a year which is nothing for a good laptop.

But this is true only for my work Laptop, My personal computer continues to be an AMD Ryzen 7 PC I built with my bare hands a few years ago. It'll probably outlive any other Mac I ever get. I can just order RAM off Amazon or a new CPU and the PC will be new.

I could potentially rant about Apple for hours but this [16 year old article](https://boingboing.net/2010/04/02/why-i-wont-buy-an-ipad-and-thi.html) by Cory Doctorow has this wonderful quote I would like to add here that sums it up pretty well (It's also in the book btw)

> Buying an iPad for your kids isn't a means of jump-starting the realization that the world is yours to take apart and reassemble; it's a way of telling your offspring that even changing the batteries is something you have to leave to the professionals.


---

Finally the book was a decent read, pick it up if you want to know more about the life of this Man, whether you idolize him or despise him, you will find enough examples to support your claim either way
