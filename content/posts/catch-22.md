## About the author

This book is a work of satire mixed with a lot of exaggeration. So, before getting into the book and the story, it's good to read up on the author
Wherever you read about Joseph Heller, he comes across as a satirist, but unless you dig into who he is you won't realize he was a bombardier himself
He flew 60 combat missions as a B-25 bombardier. The absurdity, bureaucracy, and sheer terror of these missions are what provided the foundational raw material for Catch-22.

I for one learnt this after finishing the book after which my first thought was, wait is working for the Army really that ridiculous. Well, it's coming from personal experience, it probably is

## The book itself

The book begins with the intro to the circular premise of Catch-22. If you claim to be insane and not want to fly any more missions, well then that means you are sane enough to know that you are insane to fly more missions and hence you can't be insane.

Yeah I had to read that twice too.

That is how a lot of parts in the whole book read, circular analogies and exchanges that make absolutely 0 sense but painted in such a way that if repeated enough times the person questioning them stops questioning them and starts questioning themselves

Growing up(Atleast where I'm from), We were always painted this glorious picture of the Armed forces, you know "a life more than the ordinary" and all the media you're served follows through on that. From Bollywood movies to tales of bravery from the various regiments. So I always had that image in mind, this book fully challenges and flips that one on its head

Catch-22 is set on the Island of Pianosa in the Mediterranean, follows Captain Yossarian who is a bombardier for a WW-2 bomb squadron, an anti-hero who does not want to die for his country, who does not want to fly more missions.

He realizes America has almost won the war and the only reason they're being made to fly more missions is because his seniors want to look good in front of their seniors who they secretly hate.

And every mission he flies he's putting his life into even more danger just because his seniors want to look good. He is no longer dying for the country, but just so that someone else can prove how good of a leader they are and how motivated their squad is.

The book then progresses through ridiculously funny and strange tales of authoritarianism, funny exchanges and incidents.

At the end Yossarian does finally find a way to get grounded and sent back home, even get a promotion but the catch is that he can't bad-mouth anyone at the camps once he goes back home. He has to sing praises of the same seniors who treated him as a number on the roster. If he doesn't, they will court-martial and then execute him in the interest of national security ??

He decides to refuse the offer and eventually learns that one of his closest crew-mates who was suspected dead has finally escaped the cog by just faking his death and running away and the book closes with him trying to do the same.

There are also fun little ridiculous absurd cynical side plots like Milo bombing his own squadron for the Germans for some money and then somehow convincing his seniors that it was actually beneficial for them and even getting a promotion for it. Or the tale of the Major whose name was Major Major

## Some fun lines / incidents from the book

* "You're the new squadron commander, But don't think it means anything because it doesn't, It just means you're the new squadron commander"
* This one incident where Colonel Cathcart has marked a line of bombs in Bologna for a mission to be flown tomorrow and Yossarian just sneaks into the tent and moves that line by a few inches inland and the next day everyone packs their things up because that region has now been captured and the troops can move in
* One incident where someone traps all the Colonels and Generals in a flat and throws away their uniforms and a general says "Well throwing our uniforms away was clever, We'll never be able to convince anyone we are superior without our uniforms"
* "We've got three CID men in the Squadron now, don't worry though they are after someone who has been calling himself Washington Irving, they're not interested in murderers"
* Nurse to an injured officer - "I suppose you just don't care if you lose your leg, do you" - "It's my leg" - "It's certainly not your leg. That leg belongs to the US Govt. It's no different than a gear or bedpan, The army has invested a lot of money to make you a pilot and you can't disobey the doctor's orders"

---

There's one part of the book that isn't funny at all.

Through the whole novel Heller keeps bringing up a mission where a gunner named Snowden died. But he never tells you what actually happened. A line here, half a sentence there. It comes up maybe a dozen times.

When you finally get the full scene, Yossarian is in the back of the plane patching a wound in Snowden's thigh. Doing a decent job of it too. But Snowden keeps saying he's cold, Yossarian opens his flak jacket and everything inside him spills out onto the floor.

Man is matter. That's Snowden's secret. Drop him out a window and he'll fall. Set fire to him and he'll burn. Bury him and he'll rot.

Every joke in the 400 pages before this hits different once you've read it.

---

Overall I am not sure if this was a tale of an overprovisioned Army like the US in WW-2 or just a tale everywhere, for now I still chose to believe the tales of bravery narrated to me with a teeny tiny(okay maybe not like teeny tiny but you get it ) slice of cynicism added to them.
